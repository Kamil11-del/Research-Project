--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"We examine the quality-speed trade-off of reverse diffusion sampling in Table 
\ref{tab:ddim_steps_sweep}. A key bottleneck of standard diffusion models is high 
inference latency due to hundreds of sequential denoising steps. In ActSSM, because 
the Mamba state-space encoder provides highly informative conditioning representations, 
the reverse DDIM trajectory converges exceptionally fast. Reducing sampling steps 
from $S=100$ to $S=25$ incurs virtually zero loss in TAS Avg (96.59\% vs. 96.55\%) while 
slashing latency by 3.6$\times$ (from 9.84s to 2.74s per video). Even at an aggressive 
$S=5$ steps, ActSSM maintains 97.02\% TAS Avg and 48.60\% LTA Avg at sub-second speeds 
(0.88s/video), enabling near-real-time temporal action understanding."

PAPER ANALYSIS & PARAGRAPH (To include in Section 4.X - Architectural Ablation):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Table \ref{tab:ablation_variants} validates our key architectural design choices:
1) \textbf{Pre- vs. Post-Encoding Masking:} Inserting mask tokens after the encoder 
(V2) yields a minor gain in TAS (90.11\% vs. 89.77\%), but causes a catastrophic 
10.90\% drop in LTA (25.03\% vs. 35.93\%). This confirms that the state-space encoder 
must observe mask tokens early in the pipeline to build contextual representations 
of unobserved future frames.
2) \textbf{Dual Task-Specific Mask Tokens:} Decoupling the anticipation token 
($\tau_{ant}$) from the interpolation token ($\tau_{other}$) in V3 improves LTA 
by +2.92\% over the shared-token baseline V1 (35.93\% vs. 33.01\%) while maintaining 
top-tier segmentation performance.
3) \textbf{Impact of Positional Encodings:} Explicit sinusoidal positional encodings 
(V4, V5, V6) consistently harm performance across both tasks (e.g., V6 drops TAS to 
76.54\% and LTA to 25.93\%). Because the state-space model natively processes sequences 
via causal differential equations, explicit positional additions inject high-frequency 
noise that disrupts the SSM's recurrent dynamics."

"Task- and Noise-Adaptive Conditioning in Unified Diffusion Models for Action Segmentation and Anticipation"

We identify two sources of conditioning misspecification in existing unified TAS/LTA 
diffusion models: (1) a single shared mask token conflates two semantically distinct 
missingness types, producing gradient interference in joint training; and (2) fixed 
cross-conditioning gate strength is blind to diffusion noise level, applying 
identical encoder trust at all denoising stages. We address both with a unified 
Adaptive Conditioning mechanism: task-specific mask tokens decouple TAS/LTA gradients, 
and a timestep-modulated Cross-Mamba gate adapts encoder reliance to denoising 
confidence. We provide empirical gradient analysis confirming (1) and ablative 
evidence for (2).
